How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

A water heater swap seems easy right up until you count the connections. A gas unit needs a leak-free gas connection with a sediment trap, a vent that is sized and pitched correctly so carbon monoxide goes outside, and combustion air it can actually reach. Electric units need a breaker and circuit that match the element wattage. Every tank needs a correctly rated relief valve with a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps under local code. Going tankless or switching to a heat pump adds even more: bigger gas lines, different vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

Professional service handles the full job, not just the tank. We size the heater by first-hour rating or flow rate, verify the fuel, venting, and electrical capacity, and add the expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs. Every gas and water joint is leak-tested, gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check, and the old heater is hauled away. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. If your current tank still has good years left and just needs a part, we will tell you directly.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
  • Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safety and slower mineral buildup
  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
