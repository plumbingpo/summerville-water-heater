How Charleston Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many premature tank failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the month and year it was built, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.
