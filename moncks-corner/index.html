Keeping Your Moncks Corner Water Heater Running Longer

Many early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, rumbling, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a flooded utility room.

A water heater in Moncks Corner rarely fails out of nowhere. Left alone, they lead to a failed tank and water on the floor. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon.
