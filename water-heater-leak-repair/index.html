Water around a water heater is one of the most common calls we get, and the first question is always the same: where is it actually coming from? Water travels along pipes and collects wherever the floor dips, so the puddle is often nowhere near the source. It might be the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge line, the drain valve near the bottom of the tank, the inlet and outlet fittings on top, condensation on a cold tank, or a nearby pipe that has nothing to do with the heater. It might also be the tank itself, and that answer changes everything. In Summerville, SC, we start every leak call by finding the true source.

Finding the source is a process, not a glance. We clear and dry the area, then watch where new water appears. The relief valve and its discharge pipe get checked first, followed by the drain valve and the inlet and outlet fittings, where dielectric unions and flex connectors tend to corrode. If the heater is gas-fired, we look inside the burner compartment, since water there can be condensation or a sign the tank has given out. When the relief valve is involved, we measure water pressure and check whether expansion has anywhere to go. We recommend nothing until the source is confirmed.

Most leaks are fixed in a single visit. We replace worn relief valves, swap plastic drain valves for sturdier brass valves, rebuild corroded connections, add or recharge an expansion tank, and install a pressure-reducing valve when street pressure is the real culprit. If the tank itself is leaking, there is no safe patch, and you will hear that from us directly along with your replacement options. We also help prevent the next leak from doing damage: a drain pan piped to a proper drain, a water sensor or automatic shutoff valve, and a clear label on the shutoff. How to Tell Something Is Wrong

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Moisture or mineral crust at the end of the relief valve pipe
  • Warped baseboards or stained drywall beside the water heater
  • Water collecting under or beside the heater
  • A drain valve at the bottom of the tank that drips even when closed
  • The water meter moving when nothing in the house is running
  • Rusty or discolored hot water paired with dampness at the base
  • Mineral buildup or rust around the hot and cold connections
  • Water inside the burner compartment of a gas heater

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Leak Repair

A leak looks like a simple job until you try to find it. Water can start at a top fitting, run down under the jacket, and show up at the base, and sweating tanks add confusion. Plugging a dripping relief valve removes the protection against excess pressure and heat, forcing a corroded connection can split it open, and electric heaters add 240-volt power to the mix. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Draining sediment every year helps the bottom of the tank last longer
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years; once it is used up, the tank starts to corrode
  • A quick monthly look at the base, valves, and top connections catches leaks early
  • Install a drain pan piped to a safe spot, which matters most for attic, closet, and upstairs heaters
  • A battery leak alarm beside the heater costs little and can catch a problem days sooner
  • High street pressure is hard on water heaters, so a pressure test and regulator can prevent repeat drips
  • Make sure a closed plumbing system has a working expansion tank to absorb heated water
