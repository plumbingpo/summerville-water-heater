Electric water heaters fail in a few very specific ways, and the symptom usually tells you where to look. No hot water anywhere suggests a tripped breaker, a tripped high-limit reset, or a failed upper element or upper thermostat, because the upper side controls when the lower side gets power. A tank that delivers a few minutes of hot water and then runs lukewarm suggests the lower element or lower thermostat. Water that comes out scalding points to a thermostat stuck closed or an element shorted to the tank, which is also what trips the red reset button. Warning Signs to Watch For

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A hissing or sizzling noise near the lower element
  • A tank that has stopped heating completely
  • Hot water that runs out after just a few minutes
  • Rust-colored hot water or moisture around the heater
  • The water heater breaker tripping over and over
  • The high-limit reset popping again after you push it
  • Tap water much hotter than the thermostat setting
  • Scorch marks, melted insulation, or a hot smell at the wiring

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • After any draining, refill the tank fully and run a hot faucet before restoring power
  • Test the temperature and pressure relief valve once a year
  • Protect your elements from hard water with a softener or upgraded low-watt-density elements
  • Drain a few gallons from the bottom of the tank periodically to control sediment
  • Replace a worn anode rod before corrosion moves on to the tank itself
  • Choose a setting of about 120 degrees to balance comfort, safety, and operating cost
  • Treat a tripped high-limit as a warning sign, not a nuisance
