Most tankless problems announce themselves the same way, with a blinking code on the display and a shower that turns cold. Behind that code is a system with very little margin for error. There is no storage tank to fall back on, so the heater has to detect flow, light a burner or power its elements, and raise the water temperature by fifty degrees or more in the few seconds it takes water to cross the heat exchanger. When scale narrows the passages, when the flow sensor stops reading, when the flame rod cannot confirm a flame, or when the vent or gas line cannot keep up, the unit protects itself by shutting down. That is the pattern we see again and again in Summerville, SC.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • Small draws, like a bathroom sink, that never trigger the heater
  • Water that swings between hot and lukewarm in the middle of a shower
  • An error code flashing on the unit's display
  • Cold slugs in the middle of hot water that last longer than before
  • Repeated ignition attempts, then a shutdown
  • Temperature dropping sharply when a second shower or the dishwasher starts

Small Symptoms, Big Consequences

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. Don't wait. Get Summerville Water Heater on the phone at (843) 418-2790 and we'll get a technician scheduled.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Tankless heaters look simple from the outside, but inside is a tightly controlled system. A single error code can come from four or five different faults, and the sensors, igniters, and control boards involved are not cheap to buy on a hunch. Gas models add real hazards: the burner can fire at close to 200,000 BTU per hour, the vent carries combustion gases that contain carbon monoxide, and a loose gas connection or a vent joint that separates can put fuel or exhaust into the living space. Electric tankless units carry their own risk, often drawing well over 100 amps across several 240-volt circuits, so a loose lug or undersized wire can overheat quickly.

What you get from a professional is certainty. Instead of swapping parts, our technicians confirm the fault with a meter, a manometer, and temperature readings, so the fix is aimed at the actual cause. Scale is removed with a pump through the service valves rather than vinegar poured in and left to chance. Gas pressure is checked with the burner at full output, vent runs are inspected against the manufacturer's length and clearance limits, and the unit is tested at low and high flow before we leave. Proper service also keeps you in line with the manufacturer's warranty terms, many of which expect qualified service and regular descaling.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Have the unit descaled at least once a year, or more often if your water is hard
  • Do not keep resetting a recurring code; note it and have it checked
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
  • If scale comes back quickly, add a softener or a scale-prevention cartridge on the cold inlet
  • Make sure nothing blocks the exhaust vent or the air intake outside
  • Never cut power to an outdoor or garage unit in winter, because it needs electricity to protect itself from freezing
  • Tell us before adding a gas appliance, so the gas line can be checked for the extra load
