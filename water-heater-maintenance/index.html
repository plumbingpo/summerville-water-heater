Two quiet processes decide how long a water heater lasts: sediment and corrosion. Sediment comes from minerals that separate out as water is heated and sink to the bottom of the tank, where they steal efficiency, cause popping and rumbling on gas models, and overheat the steel or bury the lower element on electric ones. Corrosion is held back by the anode rod, a sacrificial rod of magnesium or aluminum that gives itself up so the tank lining is spared. Nobody sees either process happening, and by the time a homeowner notices, the tank is often leaking. That is the reason maintenance matters for Summerville, SC homes: it interrupts both problems while they are still cheap to fix.

The work is hands-on and specific. We flush the tank until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we verify the expansion tank still holds its air charge, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless owners need maintenance too, just a different kind. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers error codes, reduced flow, or temperature swings. We flush the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Higher gas or electric costs for the same routine
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • A smell like rotten eggs that shows up only on the hot side
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A few maintenance tasks suit homeowners, and many do not. Keeping clutter away from the heater, checking for leaks, and noticing new noises are all useful. Flushing, anode replacement, relief valve testing, and burner or element work are another story. Hot water, seized fittings, brittle drain valves, and sediment that clogs the valve midway through can turn a simple job into a flooded floor. Relief valves on older heaters sometimes fail to close after testing. And gas-side checks involve flame quality and venting, which affect your household's air.

We arrive with the parts and tools the job usually needs, including drain valves, anode rods and flexible anodes, relief valves, and meters for electrical testing. Tankless descaling gear comes along for those units. Experience counts as much as equipment: an experienced eye can spot a corroding nipple, a sooty burner, or an expansion tank that has lost its charge long before any of them cause trouble. Summerville Water Heater treats maintenance as a way to catch small issues early and keep your heater working for as many years as possible.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Look around the base of the heater periodically for moisture
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often in hard water
