Many of the installation calls we get in Summerville, SC are not replacements at all. A household outgrows its tank and needs more capacity. A homeowner wants the heater out of a hall closet and into the garage or basement. A remodel calls for a second unit at the far end of the house, or an all-electric conversion means trading gas for a heat pump model. What these jobs share is new work: gas lines that must be sized for the load, vents that must be routed and pitched correctly, circuits that must match the heater, and water lines that must be run cleanly. Summerville Water Heater works all of that out before the installation starts.

Every installation starts with sizing and location. We size tanks by first-hour rating and tankless units by gallons per minute at the temperature rise your incoming water needs, so the heater keeps up with your busiest hour. Then we look at where it will go. Gas units need a way to vent outdoors and enough air for combustion. Heat pump models need room, a space that stays fairly warm, and somewhere to drain condensate. Electric tankless units can need a heavy dedicated circuit and the panel capacity behind it. A heater in an attic or above finished space needs a drain pan piped to a safe spot. We work through all of it with you in plain language, then give you a firm price.

The install itself follows a checklist: gas piping sized, run, fitted with a sediment trap, and leak-tested, vents built to the manufacturer's size and pitch, circuits and breakers matched to the heater's load, and water lines connected with shutoffs and dielectric protection where dissimilar metals meet. The relief valve gets a full-size discharge line to a safe point, and we add an expansion tank, drain pan, or seismic straps wherever code calls for them, including the permit when one is required. To finish, we fill and purge the tank, test draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, set the thermostat near 120 degrees, and walk you through the new setup. Warning Signs to Watch For

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A planned switch from gas to electric, or to a heat pump model
  • New construction or a major renovation that needs a heater from scratch
  • Missing code items like an expansion tank, drain pan, or proper venting
  • Morning showers that run cold for the last person
  • A heater squeezed into a closet or attic where a leak would do real damage
  • A new jetted tub that outpaces the heater's capacity
  • An addition too far from the existing heater for quick hot water
  • Taps at the far end of the house that take minutes to warm up

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Consider the gas side first. A new heater on an undersized line starves for fuel whenever the furnace or range is running, and a whole-house tankless unit often needs a larger line than the house already has. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
  • Plan an annual descaling if you installed a tankless unit
  • Drain a few gallons from a new tank twice a year to control sediment
  • Keep a clear path to the heater so it can be serviced and inspected easily
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater
  • A 120-degree setting protects against burns and slows mineral buildup
